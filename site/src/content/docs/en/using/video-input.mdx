Pick the source under **Input → Video source**. One is used at a time.

## DJI goggles

For **Goggles 2, Integra, Goggles 3 and N3**. Connect them to the Pi's
**USB-C** port with a USB-C data cable. You get the video, the air unit's
[camera settings](/docs/using/camera-settings), the goggles' battery and the link
quality.

The video is the goggles' own H.264 stream. YStreamer passes it on as it is,
without encoding it again.

Some links carry H.265 instead. YStreamer works out which one it gets, and
converts H.265 to H.264 once, on the Pi's hardware decoder and encoder, so
every output keeps working. **Input → Video source** shows which codec
arrived and how it's converted. Without the hardware decoder it decodes on the
CPU, and at worst converts everything on the CPU at 720p.

<Admonition type="tip" title="No OSD in the feed">
  To keep the OSD out of the video, turn off **Camera View Recording** in the
  goggles, under Settings → Camera → Advanced Camera Settings.
</Admonition>

### Radio region

**Input → Radio region** sets the transmit limits the goggles and the aircraft
follow, the same region choice DJI's own apps make by country: **FCC** (as in
the United States), **CE** (as in Europe, the radios' factory setting) or
**Indonesia**.

<Admonition type="warning" title="Your local radio rules">
  Transmit-power limits differ by country. You're responsible for following your
  local radio rules in the region you pick. Turning the setting on asks you to
  confirm this first.
</Admonition>

It's off by default, and off leaves the radios as DJI's apps last set them.
When it's on:

- The region goes to the goggles' and the aircraft's transceivers each time
  the goggles connect, and again when an aircraft links. It's never sent to the
  flight controller.
- A short repeat follows every 2 seconds while the goggles are connected,
  because the radios drop back to CE without it. CE itself needs no repeat.
- **Send again** sends the whole set once more, for example after swapping
  the aircraft.

Turning it off puts CE back, but only if this unit set a region before. A
region set some other way is left alone. If the aircraft isn't linked at that
moment, CE goes to it the next time it is.

<Admonition type="info" title="Not flight-tested yet">
  This way of setting the region hasn't been flight-tested yet. Check the
  transmission range on the ground before relying on it.
</Admonition>

## DJI RC-N2 / RC-N3 remote

For aircraft flown with DJI Fly and an **RC-N2 or RC-N3** remote, such as the
Neo 2 or the Mini series. Connect the remote's USB-C port (the one a phone
uses) to the Pi's **USB-C** port, and keep the **DJI goggles / RC-N** source.
YStreamer stands in for the phone and shows the live view. The remote sends
H.265, which is converted to H.264 as described above.

<Admonition type="tip" title="Plug the remote in last">
  The remote sends the settings its video needs only once, when the cable goes
  in. If YStreamer restarted with the remote already plugged in, it reconnects
  the USB link once by itself; if the picture still doesn't come, the Input page
  asks you to unplug the remote's cable and plug it back in.
</Admonition>

### Two-camera aircraft

An aircraft with two cameras, such as the **Avata 360**, sends both over the
remote's link. One goes to the outputs: HDMI, the browser, the streams and the
recordings. Once YStreamer sees the second camera, the Input page shows a
**Cameras** card with what each one is sending, and lets you choose the
**Camera on the outputs**:

| Choice | What goes out                                                                                        |
| ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Auto   | The bigger stream, which is the live view. It follows it when DJI Fly's picture-in-picture moves it. |
| Main   | Always the link's first camera stream.                                                               |
| Second | Always the other one.                                                                                |

Saving the choice switches the camera at once, without restarting anything. The
switch waits for the other camera's next keyframe, so the picture never mixes
the two.

<Admonition type="info" title="Only once armed">
  The aircraft sends its second camera only once it's armed. On the bench the
  card doesn't appear yet.
</Admonition>

<Admonition type="warning" title="The picture size can change">
  The two cameras have different sizes, for example 1920 × 1080 and 640 × 360. A
  switch during a recording or a stream changes the picture size mid-way, which
  some players and streaming sites don't handle.
</Admonition>

## DJI Fly passthrough

With the DJI goggles source, an Android phone running **DJI Fly** can go in one
of the Pi's **USB-A** ports. YStreamer passes the goggles' link through to it,
so you keep DJI Fly's camera settings, maps and flight view while YStreamer
streams and records. What you change in DJI Fly goes back to the goggles.

<Admonition type="warning" title="Experimental">
  Passthrough hasn't been tried on real hardware by the maintainers yet.
</Admonition>

It's on by default, under **Input → DJI Fly passthrough**, and does nothing
until an Android phone is plugged in.

1. Plug the phone into a USB-A port with a data cable.
2. The phone asks which app should open the accessory. Choose **DJI Fly**.
3. The card says **DJI Fly is connected.**

The card shows what it sees:

- **No phone**: plug in an Android phone with DJI Fly.
- **Phone connected, waiting for DJI Fly**: open DJI Fly, or answer the dialog
  on the phone. If the phone was locked when you plugged it in, YStreamer
  offers it to the phone again after about half a minute.
- **DJI Fly is connected.**

If you plug phones into the Pi just to charge them, turn the switch off and
**Save**: while it's on, an Android phone is switched into accessory mode and
asks to open DJI Fly. A phone sharing its internet over USB is left alone.

<Admonition type="info" title="Android only">
  iPhone isn't supported: accessories for it need Apple's licensed MFi program.
</Admonition>

## FPV Goggles V1 / V2

The first DJI FPV Goggles. Connect them to one of the Pi's **USB-A** ports.
Video only: no camera settings, battery or link quality.

<Admonition type="warning" title="Experimental">
  This source hasn't been tried on real hardware by the maintainers yet.
</Admonition>

The card shows what it sees:

- **Receiving video.**
- **Goggles found, no video yet**: power the aircraft and wait for the
  picture in the goggles.
- **No goggles found**: check the cable and the port.

## USB camera

Webcams, HDMI capture sticks and analog receivers, in one of the **USB-A**
ports.

| Setting            | What it does                                                             |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Device             | Which camera. **First one found** uses whichever is plugged in.          |
| Resolution         | A mode the camera offers. **Best available** goes up to 1080p at 30 fps. |
| Bitrate (kbit/s)   | 500 to 20000. About 6000 for 1080p, 2500 for an analog receiver.         |
| Keyframe every (s) | 1 to 10. Shorter means players start sooner after joining.               |

**Refresh** looks for cameras again after you plug one in.

The camera's picture is encoded to H.264 by the Pi's hardware encoder. A
camera that sends H.264 itself is passed through as it is, and its own
bitrate applies.

<Admonition type="info" title="Software encoding">
  If the hardware encoder isn't available, the Input page says so and encoding
  falls back to the CPU, at 720p at most. That's usually too little video
  memory: add `gpu_mem=256` to `/boot/firmware/config.txt` and reboot. The image
  and the install script already set it.
</Admonition>
